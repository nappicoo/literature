I Introduction

Introduction

Good morning, dear teacher and fellow students. Today we are going to talk about the Neoclassical Age in English literature — an important period in literary history.

During this time, writers valued reason, order, and knowledge. They believed literature should not only entertain, but also teach valuable lessons about life and society.

Let us explore this fascinating period and discover what made it so important.

II Historical Background

Historical Background

1660
1798

The Neoclassical Age lasted approximately from 1660 to 1798 — a time of great political, scientific and cultural change in England.

The word “Neoclassical” means “new classical.” Writers admired the literature of ancient Greece and Rome and followed their ideas about order, balance and reason.

People became more interested in science, education and logical thinking — ideas that strongly influenced the literature of this period.

V Importance

Importance of the Neoclassical Age

The Neoclassical Age changed the way people understood literature. Writers used poetry, essays, plays and satire to express ideas and discuss problems in society.

They encouraged readers to think critically, question human behavior, and learn from their mistakes.

This period also prepared the way for the Romantic Age, when writers began to give greater attention to emotion, imagination and nature.

As a result, the Neoclassical Age became an important bridge between different literary movements.

VI Conclusion

To Conclude

The Neoclassical Age was a remarkable period that emphasized reason, order and moral values. Its writers believed literature could do more than entertain — it could teach us, challenge our thinking, and help us understand human nature.
